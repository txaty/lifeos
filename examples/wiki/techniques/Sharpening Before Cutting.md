# Sharpening Before Cutting

## What it is for

Recognising when the difficulty of a task is actually the difficulty of a tool, and
fixing the tool first.

## Steps

1. When something feels harder than it should, ask what is doing the cutting.
2. Spend a deliberate, uninterrupted block improving that, producing nothing.
3. Return to the original task and re-measure.

## When it works

- When the tool is used repeatedly. The sharpening amortises.
- When the difficulty is consistent rather than occasional.

## When it does not

- When the task is one-off: sharpening costs more than the cut is worth.
- When "sharpening" becomes a way of not starting. There is a real failure mode
  where the tools are always one improvement away from ready.
