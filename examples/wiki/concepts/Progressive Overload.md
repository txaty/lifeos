# Progressive Overload

## What it is

Capacity grows when load rises slightly faster than the body has already adapted to,
and then is allowed to recover. Too little load and nothing changes; too much and the
tissue fails before it adapts. The useful band is narrower and lower than it feels.

## Why it matters

It explains why the common failure is not laziness but impatience, and it reframes
rest from "the absence of training" to "the part where the training takes effect".

## Key points

- Roughly 10% more volume per week is the usual ceiling for running.
- Recovery is when adaptation happens, so a skipped rest day costs more than a
  skipped session.
- Easy days drifting harder is the quiet way a plan stops working.

## In practice

- Cap weekly increases and hold the cap even in a good week.
- Keep easy runs conversational. If in doubt, slower.
- Two easy days between hard days.

## Disagreements

> The article argues the ceiling is ~10% per week for everyone. Robin Hale's
> experience ([[Robin Hale]], 2026-08-24) is that trained runners returning after a
> break tolerate considerably more. Both may be true — the ceiling is probably a
> function of prior adaptation, not a constant — but nothing here settles it.
