# Notes on Habit Formation

<!-- A `note` source is your own written-up thinking, captured so it can be cited
     like any other source. Written once, then compiled. -->

## Summary

Thinking out loud after three weeks of a training plan actually sticking, when
several previous attempts did not.

## Key points

- What changed was not motivation. It was that the run is now attached to an
  existing fixed point in the day rather than to a decision.
- Every habit I have kept is attached to something that was already happening.
- Every habit I have lost required me to decide, each time, that now was the moment.
- Reducing the frequency made it more likely to survive, not less.

## Content

The interesting part is that lowering the target increased adherence. Six days a
week required a decision every day about whether today counted. Four days a week
with fixed slots required no decisions at all. The plan got easier and the outcome
got better, which suggests the binding constraint was decision-making, not effort.
