# Base Training for Distance Runners

<!-- IMMUTABLE. Written once by ingestion; never edited afterwards. -->

## Summary

An argument for spending the first two thirds of any distance plan at conversational
pace. The author's claim is that adaptation is limited by how much running the body
can absorb, not by how hard any single session was, and that most amateur plans
fail because their easy days are moderately hard.

## Key points

- Volume tolerance builds slowly; roughly 10% more per week is the usual ceiling.
- Easy runs should be genuinely easy — conversational, not merely "not a race".
- Two consecutive hard days is the most common route to a calf or achilles problem.
- Adaptation happens during recovery, so a missed rest day costs more than a missed run.

## Content

Most training plans are written as if the limiting factor were willpower. It is not.
The limiting factor is how much load the tissue can absorb before it starts to fail,
and that number rises slowly and falls quickly.

The practical consequence is unglamorous. Run more, run slower, and treat the easy
days as the part of the plan that is actually doing the work. The hard sessions are
seasoning. If the easy days creep up in pace — and they always do, because they feel
too easy — the whole structure quietly turns into a series of moderate days, which
develops nothing and injures reliably.
